How a sourcing request runs.

One written path from your specification to a loaded container. It applies to every line except castor oil, which runs on a live, dated mill price instead.

The six steps

  1. You send the specification. Product, grade, quantity, packing preference and destination port. One product and one destination is enough to start.
  2. We check the factory. Site details, direct contact, grade and packing verified before any name is shared. A directory listing is discovery, not proof.
  3. You get a project quote. Grade, packing, port, lead time and validity in one document. Default validity is 30 days, shorter where the market moves.
  4. Sample approved. A pre-shipment sample goes against the agreed specification. Sample and factory verification are free.
  5. Loading and documents. The lot ships from the mill. Per-lot COA, third-party inspection notes where the line supports it, and HS codes travel with it.
  6. We invoice after loading. On the bill of lading date, once the container is on board. Nothing is payable before that.

Two rules that do not move

  • One side pays. You or the mill, never both on the same order. The paying side is fixed in writing before any commitment.
  • No fee on a failed shipment. Cancelled after loading, or failed pre-shipment inspection: nothing is due.

Pass-through costs — lab tests, factory audits, pre-shipment inspections — are billed at the supplier invoice, with the invoice attached, and only when you ask for them.
